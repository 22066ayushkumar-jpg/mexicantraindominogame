Dominoes vs Mexican Train: Key Differences and Rules

Dominoes vs Mexican Train comes down to how many friends you have at your table and how much time you want to spend playing. Both games use rectangular tiles with dots called pips. Classic Dominoes is a fast, quiet game for two to four players using a small box of 28 tiles. Mexican Train is a loud, social game for up to eight players using 91 colorful tiles, a center hub, and plastic train markers. How to Play: Dominoes vs Mexican Train

Dominoes vs Mexican Train starts with shuffling the tiles face down on the table, which players call the boneyard. In Classic Dominoes, each player takes seven tiles, and the person with the highest double puts it in the center. Players take turns matching one of their tiles to the two open ends of the chain. In Mexican Train, the round's matching double (starting with Double-12) goes inside the plastic center hub. Players draw a set number of tiles and build their own personal line outward from that center hub. That is the core setup for Dominoes vs Mexican Train.

Strategy: Dominoes vs Mexican Train

Dominoes vs Mexican Train requires two different ways of thinking to beat your friends. In Classic Dominoes, you count which tiles are already on the table so you can figure out what numbers your opponent does not have. Then you play numbers that block them from moving. In Mexican Train, your main goal is getting rid of heavy high-dot tiles early before someone ends the round. You also want to hold onto a double tile to trap opponents and force their private trains open. Good planning wins games in Dominoes vs Mexican Train.

Rules: Dominoes vs Mexican Train

Dominoes vs Mexican Train rules change what happens when you get stuck or play a double tile. In Classic Dominoes, if you have no matching tile, you draw from the pile until you find one, or you pass if the pile is empty. Doubles have no special penalty. In Mexican Train, if you cannot play after drawing one tile, you must put a plastic marker on your train. That marker lets everyone else play on your line until you can play on it yourself. Also, if you play a double in Mexican Train, you must immediately cover it with another matching tile. These two rules separate Dominoes vs Mexican Train.

How the Winner Is Chosen: Dominoes vs Mexican Train

Dominoes vs Mexican Train flips the scoreboard upside down when picking the final winner. A hand ends in both games when one player runs out of tiles, or when nobody can make a move. In Classic Dominoes, the person who empties their hand wins all the dot points left in everyone else's hands. The first person to hit 100 points wins the match. In Mexican Train, you count the dots left in your own hand at the end of each round and write them down as penalty points. After 13 rounds, the person with the lowest score wins Dominoes vs Mexican Train.
