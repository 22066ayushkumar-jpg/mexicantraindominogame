How to Play Mexican Train Dominoes: Official Rules & Setup

Mexican Train Dominoes is a modern classic: a domino game for 2 to 4 players where everyone builds their own "train" of dominoes from a shared engine, plus one public Mexican Train that anyone can build on. You win a round by playing all of your tiles — and win the game by scoring the fewest pips across all rounds. Here are the complete, official rules.

New to the game? The fastest way to learn is to play a free game online with the rules on this page open next to you — the computer players will show you how a first turn works in minutes.

What you need

  • A domino set. The classic choice is a double-twelve set (91 dominoes) for up to four players. Double-six, double-nine and double-fifteen sets also work — see the table below.
  • A center "hub" and something to mark open trains with — a coin, a small toy train, anything distinctive per player.
  • Paper and pencil for scoring — or our free printable Mexican Train score sheet.

Setup

  1. Shuffle the dominoes face down. This pool is the boneyard.
  2. Draw your hands. Each player takes the number of dominoes shown for your set and player count below. Keep your hand hidden.
  3. Find the engine. The highest double in anyone's hand becomes the engine — for example 12|12. That player places the engine in the center hub and takes the first turn.
  4. Organize your train. Each player's personal train grows outward from the hub; the Mexican Train also starts from the hub. Until someone starts the Mexican Train, it's empty.

Leftover dominoes stay in the boneyard for drawing. With a double-six set and four players there is no boneyard — if you can't play, you simply pass.

Your turn

Play moves clockwise. On your turn you may play one domino on any train you're allowed to build on:

  • Your personal train — always open to you. Its first domino must match the engine's number; after that, match the open end.
  • The Mexican Train — open to every player, every turn. Its first domino must also match the engine.
  • Other players' trains — only when they are marked open (see below).

To play a domino, one of its two ends must match the open end of the train, exactly like 3|4 – 4|9 – 9|1. Play your domino so the matching end touches the train.

If you can't play: draw, then open your train

  1. If you have no legal play, draw one domino from the boneyard.
  2. If the drawn domino plays, you may play it immediately.
  3. If it doesn't — or the boneyard is empty — your train is marked open (place your marker on it) and your turn ends.

An open train is an invitation: any other player may build on it until you play a domino on it again, which closes it. Leaving your train open is bad — it lets opponents unload heavy dominoes on your train instead of their own.

Doubles must be covered

A double (like 9|9) is placed sideways across its train, and it must be covered:

  • If you play a double, you get a bonus play: immediately play a second domino matching the double's number on it.
  • Stuck covering your own double? Your train gets flagged open, and the duty to match it rolls to the next player at the table.
  • Anyone else who comes up short — even after a draw — flags their own train too, and the duty keeps rolling around the table until somebody finally matches it.
  • While a double is uncovered, its train can't be extended past it — covering it is the only play on that train.

Ending a round and scoring

  • The round ends the moment a player plays their last domino — or when every player is blocked and no dominoes remain to draw.
  • The player who went out scores 0. In a blocked round, the hand carrying the fewest pips escapes penalty.
  • Everyone else scores the total pips (dots) left in their hand.
  • Play another round — three is the tradition. When the last round is done, the smallest accumulated pip total takes the match.

Quick strategy pointers

  • A closed personal train starves your opponents — with nowhere to unload, their pip count climbs instead of yours.
  • Unload your heaviest dominoes early; every pip counts against you at the end of a round.
  • Hold doubles until you can cover them, or play them when they help you shed two tiles in one turn.
