Mexican Train Dominoes Strategy: Opening Moves, Doubles & Tips to Win

Like most classic games, winning at Mexican Train Dominoes mixes luck and skill. You can't control which dominoes you draw — but you can control which ones you play, when, and where. These strategies are exactly what the hard computer opponents on this site use against you.

1. The best opening moves

  • Start your personal train first. Your train is the only one you can always build on, so get it running before you help the Mexican Train.
  • Unload heavy dominoes early. If two dominoes both fit, play the one with more pips. A 11|5 played now can't hurt you later; a 11|5 stuck in your hand is 16 points.
  • Keep a matching domino back. Before playing a tile, glance at your hand: if nothing else matches the end you're about to create, you may be setting yourself up to draw next turn.
Opening tip: some opening moves are simply better than others — the strongest first play keeps your options open while telling you nothing about whether your train will need to open.

2. Protect your train, borrow everyone else's

  • Your personal train closed means opponents can't dump their pips on it. Play on your own train whenever the plays are equal.
  • The Mexican Train is neutral ground — use it to shed tiles without helping any single opponent too much.
  • When a rival's train opens, it's a gift: put your heaviest remaining dominoes there, not on your own train.
  • Watch opponents' tile counts. When someone is down to one or two dominoes, play defensively: keep your train closed and avoid starting new branches they can use.

3. The best times to play a double

Doubles are the highest-leverage dominoes in the game — and the most dangerous:

  • Play a double when you can cover it. A covered double is two dominoes gone in one turn, and doubles carry big pip counts — getting rid of 10|10 and its cover in one turn is a huge swing.
  • Never play a double you can't cover unless the double itself is worth more than the risk. An uncovered double opens your train and hands the bonus play to whoever can cover it.
  • Save one cover tile. If you hold the only domino that can cover your own double, you control the timing — play the pair when it empties you fastest.
  • Cover other players' doubles reluctantly. Sometimes it's required — but covering burns a matching tile you might need for your own train.
Rule of thumb: a double is a two-tile turn. If you don't have the second tile ready, you're playing a one-tile turn that opens your train.

4. Count your pips

Score is pips left in your hand, so think in points, not tiles:

  • A hand of 11|11, 9|9 and 6|6 is fewer dominoes but more points than five low blanks-and-ones.
  • Getting rid of heavy dominoes is worth more than getting rid of many light ones.
  • Late in a round, choose the play that leaves the fewest pips in your hand even if it keeps more tiles — if the round ends blocked, the lowest pip count scores zero.

5. Endgame: block, or run?

  • If an opponent is about to go out, try to end the round blocked instead — especially if your pip count is the lowest at the table.
  • If you are about to go out, keep your last dominoes flexible: hold dominoes that match several different numbers.
  • Remember: the round ends the instant someone plays their last tile. Every turn you leave them able to play is a turn they might win the round.

6. Common beginner mistakes

  • Playing on the Mexican Train out of habit while your own train sits closed and useless to you.
  • Hoarding doubles "for later" until they're the heaviest thing left in your hand.
  • Reopening your own train by forgetting to keep a tile that matches its end.
  • Ignoring opponents' tile counts — the player at two dominoes should change everyone's plan.
