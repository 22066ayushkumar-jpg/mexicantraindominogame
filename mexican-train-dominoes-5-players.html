Mexican Train Dominoes 5 Players: Rules, Setup & Scoring

Last updated 24 October 2026 · Table rules for 5 players · Double-12 set

Mexican Train dominoes with 5 players uses the standard double-12 rules: every player draws 12 dominoes, then takes turns building a line of matching tiles — a train — outward from a central double domino. The goal is to empty your hand first, leave your opponents holding points, and finish the 13-round game with the lowest total score.

Playing online? The free browser game on this site seats 2 to 4 players and starts each round with the highest double dealt. The rules below are the standard five-player table version — use the free online Mexican Train game for smaller groups, and this page when you have five at the table.

What you need to play Mexican Train with 5 players

Gather these supplies before sitting down:

  • A double-12 domino set: the standard set for five players. It has 91 tiles numbered from 0 (blank) up to 12. The dots on each half of a tile are called pips.
  • A central hub: most Mexican Train sets include a plastic or wooden centerpiece. It holds the starting double domino in the middle and has slots around the outside to keep everyone's train organized. Without a hub, arrange the tiles around the center domino on the bare table.
  • 6 train markers: you need one small marker for each of the five players, plus one distinct marker for the public Mexican Train. Sets come with plastic train pieces, though pennies and a nickel work too.
  • A flat playing surface: a dining table gives five players enough room for their private hands, personal trains, and the shared draw pile.
  • Paper and a pen: one person keeps score and records everyone's points at the end of each round. Our printable Mexican Train score sheet has room for five players.

How many dominoes does each player get with 5 players?

With five players, each person draws 12 dominoes from a standard double-12 set.

Before anyone draws, pull the round's starting double domino — the engine — out of the pile and put it in the center hub. Turn the remaining 90 dominoes face down and shuffle them. Once all five players draw 12 tiles (60 tiles total), push the 30 leftover face-down tiles to the side to form the boneyard (the draw pile).

While 12 tiles is the standard starting hand in most modern sets, a few older rulebooks deal 14 tiles for five players. Stick to 12 tiles so the boneyard stays large enough to keep rounds from locking up early.

How to set up Mexican Train dominoes

1. Choose the engine domino

A full game runs for 13 rounds, and each round uses a specific double domino (a tile with matching numbers on both ends) as its engine. Round 1 starts with the Double-12 (12-12). Round 2 uses the Double-11 (11-11), Round 3 uses the Double-10 (10-10), and so on down to the Double-0 (blank-blank) in Round 13. If your group prefers a shorter match or a different engine rule, see the Mexican Train variations guide.

2. Place the engine in the center

Pull the Double-12 tile out of the set and snap it into the center hub before shuffling. Taking the engine out first saves players from digging through their hands to find it.

3. Prepare the personal trains

Shuffle the remaining 90 tiles face down. Have all five players draw 12 dominoes and stand them on edge — or place them on racks — so opponents cannot see the pips. Each player claims the hub slot directly in front of them for their personal train. Leave one empty slot on the hub for the shared Mexican Train, and keep all six train markers near the center of the table.

4. Decide who goes first

Before dealing Round 1 hands, have everyone draw one tile from the boneyard; the player with the highest pip count goes first, and those tiles are shuffled back in. You can also let the youngest player start or flip a coin. Starting in Round 2, the first turn rotates one seat clockwise each round.

How to play Mexican Train dominoes with 5 players

Play moves clockwise around the table. In standard rules, you play one domino per turn by connecting it to an eligible train.

Start your personal train

To start your personal train in Round 1, place a domino from your hand into your hub slot so the end touching the hub matches the engine. Because Round 1 starts with the Double-12, your first domino must have a 12 on one side, such as a 12-7. Put the 12 end against the hub so the 7 end points outward.

Match the open end of a train

Every domino placed after the first tile must match the open end (the unconnected half) of the train you play on. If your personal train ends with that 12-7 tile, the 7 is the open end. On your next turn, you can play a 7-4 by placing the 7 against the 7, making the 4 the new open end. Dominoes form a single chain and never branch sideways, though you can curve the line so it fits on the table.

Play on the Mexican Train

The Mexican Train is a public train open to everyone. Any player on any turn can start the Mexican Train by placing a tile that matches the central engine (a 12 in Round 1) into an empty slot on the hub. Set the Mexican Train marker next to this line so everyone knows it is public. Once started, any of the five players can play a matching domino on the open end of the Mexican Train during their turn instead of playing on their own train. Only one Mexican Train is allowed per round.

Play on another player's open train

Your personal train is private unless it has a marker on it. If a player cannot make a move and passes their turn, they must put their train marker on the end of their personal train. A train with a marker on it is an open train (or public train). While that marker sits on the train, any of the five players can add matching dominoes to it.

What happens when you cannot play?

If nothing in your hand matches your personal train, the Mexican Train, or an opponent's open train, follow these steps:

  1. Draw one domino from the boneyard.
  2. Play it right away if it matches an eligible train. Your turn ends, and you do not put a marker on your train.
  3. Keep the tile and place your marker on your personal train if the drawn domino cannot be played. Your train is now open to the table, and your turn ends. (If your train already has a marker on it, leave it there.)

You cannot pass or draw from the boneyard on purpose if you already hold a playable tile.

What happens after your first turn?

On later turns, you can play one domino on your personal train, the Mexican Train, or any opponent's marked train.

To remove the marker from your own train, you must play a domino on your own personal train. Adding a tile to your own line — even if you are laying down your very first engine match to start your train — lets you take your marker off and make your train private again. Playing on the Mexican Train or an opponent's open train does not remove the marker from your own train.

Mexican Train dominoes doubles rules

What is a double?

A double is a domino with the same number of pips on both ends, such as 8-8, 3-3, or 0-0. Play doubles sideways (perpendicular to the train) so everyone can spot them. Placing a double sideways does not split the train into branches.

What happens when you play a double?

Whenever you play a double, you must take an immediate bonus play and place one more domino from your hand on the same turn. You have two options for that second tile:

  • Cover the double directly: play a matching tile right onto the double you laid down. For example, if you play a 6-6 on an open 6, you can play a 6-2 against the 6-6. The double is now covered (satisfied), and your turn ends.
  • Play on a different eligible train: play your second domino on another available train, such as the Mexican Train. This leaves the 6-6 uncovered as an open double for the next player.

If you play a double and have no playable second tile in your hand, draw one domino from the boneyard. Play it if it fits the double or another eligible train. If it cannot be played, keep it, put a marker on your own personal train (even if you played the double on the Mexican Train or an opponent's train), and leave the double open.

If your follow-up tile is another double, play again until you lay down a non-double tile or run out of moves and draw.

How do you cover an open double?

When a player ends their turn leaving one or more doubles uncovered, normal play pauses. The next player in clockwise order must play a matching domino onto an open double before anyone can play elsewhere on the table. Even if the open double sits on someone's private unmarked train, that spot is open until a player covers the double.

If it is your turn to cover an open double and you have no matching tile:

  1. Draw one domino from the boneyard.
  2. Play it on the open double if it matches.
  3. If the drawn tile does not match, keep it, place your marker on your own personal train, and pass the turn to the next player in clockwise order.

Play continues around the table until every open double is covered, which restores normal turns.

Dead double exception: if all 12 non-double dominoes of a number have already been played on the board when someone lays down that number's double, zero matching tiles remain in the game to cover it. Treat that tile as a "dead double" and ignore the requirement to cover it.

Can you end the game with a double?

Yes. Under standard rules, if the last domino in your hand is a double and it fits an eligible train, you can play it to go out and end the round without covering it. Some groups require you to cover a final double to go out, so confirm this rule before Round 1.

How to score Mexican Train dominoes with 5 players

When does a round end?

A round ends in one of two ways:

  • A player goes out ("dominoes"): one player plays the last domino in their hand.
  • The game is blocked: the boneyard is empty, and all five players pass in a row because nobody holds a playable tile.

How to count the points on your remaining dominoes

When the round ends, the player with an empty hand scores 0 points. Every other player turns their remaining tiles face up, counts the total pips on their leftover dominoes, and reports that total to the scorekeeper.

If you are left holding a 12-9 (21 pips), a 6-4 (10 pips), and a 2-0 (2 pips), your score for the round is 33 points (21 + 10 + 2).

Under standard pip-counting rules, the Double-0 (blank-blank) tile has zero dots and counts as 0 points. Many groups add a 50-point penalty if you get caught holding the 0-0 at the end of a round, so agree on 0 or 50 points before starting.

How to keep score for five players

Write all five players' names across the top of a sheet of paper and list the rounds (12 down to 0) down the left side. Record everyone's points after each round and keep a running total. Our free printable score sheet is already laid out this way.

Who wins the full game?

After scoring Round 13 (the Double-0 round), add up each player's points across all rounds. The player with the lowest total score wins. If two players tie for the lowest score, the player who scored 0 points in the most rounds wins the tiebreaker.

Example of a Mexican Train turn with 5 players

Imagine five players — Alex, Maya, Jordan, Sam, and Taylor — sitting clockwise around the table in Round 1, with the Double-12 (12-12) in the center hub.

  1. Turn 1 (Alex starts a personal train): Alex holds the 12-8 tile and places the 12 end against the Double-12 engine in their hub slot. The 8 is now the open end of Alex's private train.
  2. Turn 2 (Maya starts a personal train): Maya plays a 12-5 into her slot, making 5 the open end of her private train.
  3. Turn 3 (Jordan cannot play and opens a train): Jordan holds no domino with a 12. Jordan draws one tile from the boneyard — a 9-4, which does not match the 12 engine. Jordan keeps the 9-4 and puts a train marker in their empty slot next to the 12-12 engine. Jordan's unstarted train is now open to all players, so anyone can play a 12 there.
  4. Turn 4 (Sam plays a double and leaves it open): Sam plays a 12-6 on their first turn. On the next lap around the table, Sam plays the 6-6 double sideways onto the open 6 of their 12-6 tile. Playing a double gives Sam an immediate second play. Instead of covering the 6-6, Sam starts the public Mexican Train by playing a 12-3 against the hub. That leaves the 6-6 on Sam's train as an open double.
  5. Turn 5 (Taylor must cover the open double): Taylor goes next. Because Sam left an open 6-6 double on the board, Taylor cannot play on their own train or the Mexican Train — Taylor must play a 6 on Sam's 6-6. Taylor holds a 6-11 and places the 6 end against Sam's 6-6. The double is now covered, Taylor's turn ends, and Alex takes a normal turn next.

Common Mexican Train dominoes rules mistakes

  • Forgetting to mark your own train when you fail to cover an opponent's double: if Player A leaves an open double on the Mexican Train, and Player B draws from the boneyard and still cannot cover it, Player B must put a marker on Player B's own personal train.
  • Removing your train marker after playing on the Mexican Train: you can only take the marker off your personal train when you play a domino onto your own personal train. Playing on the Mexican Train or an opponent's marked train leaves your own train marked and open.
  • Branching sideways off a double: unlike Chicken Foot, you only play one tile onto the front of a double to cover it. Never play tiles off the left and right sides of a regular double.
  • Drawing from the boneyard when you hold a legal play: you cannot hoard a playable tile for later. If a tile in your hand fits your train, the Mexican Train, or an open train, you must play it.
  • Playing multiple tiles on a regular turn: outside of the bonus play after laying down a double (or the "Fast Game" opening variation below), you only play one domino per turn.

Mexican Train dominoes rules variations

The main guide above uses the standard one-tile-per-turn ruleset with a 12-tile deal. Groups also play these common variations if everyone agrees before Round 1:

  • Starting tile counts (14 tiles for 5 players): while most sets deal 12 dominoes to five players, some older instructions deal 14 dominoes per player, leaving 20 tiles in the boneyard.
  • Selecting the first player by hand draw: instead of pulling out the Double-12 engine before shuffling, some tables shuffle all 91 tiles, deal 12 to each player, and check who drew the Double-12. If nobody holds it, players draw one tile at a time from the boneyard until someone pulls it. Setting the engine aside before dealing keeps hand sizes equal.
  • Opening turns ("Fast Game" multi-tile start): in several boxed editions, on the first turn a player starts their personal train, they can lay down a continuous chain of matching dominoes all at once instead of a single tile. After your train is started, you play one domino per turn.
  • Exposed doubles (must cover your own double): in this strict rule, if you play a double, your second domino must cover that double directly — you cannot play your second tile on a different train. If you cannot cover your own double, you draw one tile, and if it does not match, you mark your train and leave the double open for the next player.
  • Open-train markers (single-use penalty): as soon as any opponent plays a domino on your marked train, your marker comes off immediately. Under standard rules, the marker stays until you play on your own train.
  • Blocked rounds (low-hand bonus): in rare scoring variations, when a round blocks or a player goes out, the winner earns the sum of everyone else's points as a positive score. Most sets use low-score golf-style scoring instead.
  • Ending on a double: some tournament rules forbid ending a round on an uncovered double. If your last two tiles are a double and a matching cover tile, you can play both in one turn to go out; if you hold a single double left in your hand, you cannot go out until you draw a tile to cover it.

Frequently asked questions

How many dominoes does each player get with 5 players?

Each player draws 12 dominoes in a five-player game using a standard double-12 domino set. After placing the starting double engine in the center hub and dealing 60 tiles across the five players, the remaining 30 tiles stay face down in the boneyard. Some older rulebooks deal 14 dominoes per player as a variation.

Can you play Mexican Train with 5 players using a double-9 set?

Yes. A double-9 set has 55 tiles and runs for 10 rounds (Double-9 down to Double-0). Have each of the five players draw 8 or 9 dominoes to start. Dealing 8 tiles per player uses 40 tiles and leaves 14 in the boneyard after placing the engine.

Can you play on another player's train?

You can only play on another player's personal train when their train has a marker on it (meaning they could not play on an earlier turn), or when their train ends with an uncovered double you must satisfy. Otherwise, opponents' personal trains are private.

What happens if you cannot play a domino?

If you have no legal move in your hand, draw one domino from the boneyard. If the drawn tile matches your train, the Mexican Train, or an open train, play it right away. If it does not match, keep the tile, put your marker on the end of your personal train to open it to everyone, and end your turn.

Do you have to cover a double?

Yes, every double played on the board must be covered by a matching domino unless a player used that double as their final tile to end the round, or all 12 matching tiles of that number have already been played. While a double sits uncovered, nobody can play elsewhere until someone covers it.

Can you play two dominoes in one turn?

In standard play, you only play two dominoes in one turn when your first tile is a double, which requires you to immediately play a second domino. You can also play multiple tiles on your opening turn if your group uses the Fast Game variation to start personal trains.

How does Mexican Train scoring work?

At the end of each round, the player who emptied their hand scores 0 points. Every other player counts the total pips (dots) on the dominoes left in their hand and adds that number to their score. The player with the lowest combined score after the final round wins.

How many rounds are in Mexican Train?

A complete game with a double-12 set has 13 rounds, starting with the Double-12 engine in Round 1 and counting down one number each round to the Double-0 engine in Round 13. For a shorter game, agree beforehand to play 3 rounds or play only even-numbered engines.

What happens if nobody can play?

If the boneyard runs out of dominoes and all five players pass in order because nobody has a legal move, the round is blocked. The round ends immediately, and all five players count the pips left in their hands and add those points to their scores.
