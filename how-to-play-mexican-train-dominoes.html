How to Play Mexican Train Dominoes: Complete Rules & Setup Guide

Welcome to the ultimate beginner-to-advanced guide on how to play Mexican Train Dominoes! Whether you are setting up a double-12 domino set for family game night or looking to master competitive train-building strategies, this guide covers everything you need to know.

TL;DR — How to Play Mexican Train Dominoes

  • Goal: Be the first to play all your dominoes, or finish with the lowest total score after 13 rounds.
  • Setup: Shuffle 91 double-12 dominoes face down (the boneyard). Each player draws 11–15 tiles depending on player count.
  • Start: Round 1 begins with the Double-12 engine in the center hub. Each round after uses the next lowest double down to Double-0.
  • Turns: Play one tile per turn matching pips end-to-end on your private train, the shared Mexican Train, or an opponent's marked public train.
  • Doubles: Play a double perpendicularly, then you must immediately cover it with another matching tile or your train goes public.
  • Stuck? Draw one tile. If you still can't play, place a marker on your train — it becomes public and anyone can play on it.
  • Winning: After Round 13 (Double-0), the player with the lowest total pip score wins.

In one sentence: Match tiles end-to-end from a central double engine, clear your hand first, and finish with the lowest score.

Game Overview & Objective

Mexican Train Dominoes is a popular tabletop game played with a chain of matching numbered or dotted tiles (dominoes) radiating from a central station. The primary objective of the game is to empty your hand of all dominoes or score the fewest possible points over a series of rounds.

At the end of each round, any dominoes remaining in your hand count as penalty points based on their total number of dots (called pips). The player with the lowest total cumulative score after all rounds wins the match.

Equipment Required

To play standard Mexican Train Dominoes with up to 8 players, you will need the following equipment:

  • Double-12 Domino Set: A standard set of 91 dominoes with dot/pip values ranging from 0 (blank) to 12.
  • Central Station Hub: A specialized circular spacer placed in the middle of the table with slots to hold the starting tile (engine) and radiating train spokes.
  • Train Tokens / Markers: Distinct tokens (such as plastic trains, pennies, or poker chips) used to mark open "public" trains.
  • Scorepad & Pencil: For tracking cumulative player points across rounds.

Setup & Tile Distribution

1. Shuffle & Form the Boneyard

Place all 91 dominoes face down on the table and shuffle them thoroughly. The remaining pool of unpicked face-down tiles forms the boneyard.

Step-by-Step Gameplay Rules

Phase 1: Opening the Station

The first round begins with the highest double in the set—the Double-12—placed into the center slot of the hub to serve as the engine.

In a standard 13-round match, each subsequent round uses the next lowest double as the engine:

  • Round 1: [12|12]
  • Round 2: [11|11]
  • Round 3: [10|10]
  • ...
  • Round 13: [0|0] (Double Blank)

The player holding the required double engine places it in the hub and takes the first turn. If no player holds the engine, players take turns drawing 1 tile from the boneyard until it is found. See the full breakdown of who goes first in Mexican Train Dominoes, including what to do when you cannot match the engine.

Phase 2: Player First Turn

Starting clockwise from the leader, each player begins building their personal "private" train extending outward from the hub toward their seat:

  • On your first turn, you may play a continuous chain of multiple tiles from your hand, provided every tile connects logically end-to-end (matching pips) and the starting tile matches the central engine.
  • If you cannot start your train, draw 1 tile from the boneyard. If still unable to play, place a train token on your slot to mark your train as public.

Phase 3: Subsequent Main Turns

After the opening round of turns, standard turn rules apply:

  1. You may play exactly one domino per turn (unless playing a double).
  2. You can play your domino on:
    • Your own private train.
    • The shared Mexican Train.
    • Any opponent's train that is currently marked public.
  3. If you have a legal move, you MUST play. You cannot pass or hold back tiles strategically.
  4. If you cannot play: Draw 1 tile from the boneyard. If the drawn tile is playable, you may play it immediately. If not, you must mark your train as public by placing a token on the end.

The Mexican Train vs. Public & Private Trains

Understanding train eligibility is essential for mastering gameplay:

Caption: Visual representation of private player spokes, marked public lines, and the independent Mexican Train.

  • Private Train: Your personal track extending from your hub slot. Only you can play on this train as long as it remains unmarked.
  • Public Train: A player's train marked with a token because they could not make a legal play. Any player can add tiles to a public train.
  • Removing a Marker: To make your public train private again, you must play a valid domino onto your own marked train during a subsequent turn and remove the marker token.
  • The Mexican Train: An additional, independent public train that starts on an open slot or off to the side of the hub. It must begin with a tile matching the central engine. Once started, it remains public to all players for the entire round.

Special Rules: Playing & Satisfying Doubles

A tile with identical pips on both ends (e.g., [6|6]) is called a double. Doubles add high technical strategy to Mexican Train:

[6|3] | +-----+ | 6|6 | <--- Double placed perpendicularly (T-shape) +-----+ | [1|6]
  1. Placement: Doubles are played perpendicular across the train line to form a "T" shape.
  2. Satisfying the Double ("Closing/Covering"): When you play a double, you gain an immediate extra turn and must satisfy the double by playing a non-double matching tile directly onto it.
  3. Failing to Satisfy:
    • If you cannot satisfy your double from your hand, draw 1 tile from the boneyard.
    • If you still cannot play on it, place a train marker on your personal train.
    • The obligation passes clockwise: The next player in turn order must satisfy the double. If they cannot, their train becomes public, and the obligation passes to the following player.
  4. All Other Trains Blocked: While an open double exists on the board, no player can play on any other train until the double is satisfied.

Exceptions: A double does not need to be covered if it is the very last tile of that pip number left in the game, or if it is the final tile played to end the round.

Scoring & Winning Conditions

Ending a Round

A round ends immediately when:

  1. A player plays their final domino ("goes out").
  2. The boneyard is empty and no player has any legal moves left (a "blocked game").

Calculating Scores

  • The player who empties their hand receives 0 penalty points.
  • All other players sum the dots (pips) on their remaining hand tiles.
  • Special Tile Penalty: The Double-Blank tile ([0|0]) is worth 50 penalty points if caught in your hand at the end of a round!

Match Winner & Tie-Breakers

The player with the lowest cumulative point total after 13 rounds is the winner.

Tie-Breaker Rules:

  1. Player with the most 0-point rounds won.
  2. Player with the lowest non-zero single-round score.

Top 5 Winning Strategies

To boost your win rate in Mexican Train Dominoes, apply these expert tactical principles:

  1. Protect Your Private Train: Keep your train private as long as possible. Once public, opponents can dump unwanted high-pip tiles onto your track.
  2. Play Doubles on Public Lines: Playing a double on an opponent's public line forces everyone else to address it without putting your own private line at risk.
  3. Shed Heavy Pips Early: Play high-value tiles (like [12|11] or [12|10]) as early as possible so you aren't caught with heavy penalty scores.
  4. Use the Mexican Train Wisely: Utilize the Mexican Train to dispose of orphaned tiles that don't fit into your personal string.
  5. Track Spent Doubles: Keep mental track of which numbers have already been played so you can block opponents with dead-end values.

Popular Game Variations

  • Partnerships: Played in 2-person teams sitting opposite each other. Teammates share a single connected train and tally combined scores.
  • Branching Doubles: Satisfied doubles can branch out into four directions (crossroads) like the domino game Chicken Foot.
  • Fast Game / Simultaneous Start: Players build their initial personal train simultaneously during turn one to accelerate early play.
  • Bullet Train: A player who finishes their entire hand on their private train in a single run declares a "Bullet Train" to end the match quickly.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What happens if you run out of tiles in the boneyard?

If the boneyard is empty, players skip drawing and must pass their turn if they do not hold a legal move, placing a marker on their train.

Can you play on the Mexican Train during your first turn?

Under standard rules, players build out their personal train during turn one. The Mexican Train is typically opened on or after the second round of turns.

What happens if you only have one tile left in your hand?

When holding a single remaining domino, you must tap the table twice or announce "one tile left" to alert opponents. Failing to announce it may incur a draw penalty under house rules.
