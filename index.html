How to play Mexican Train Dominoes

  1. Match the engine. The highest double starts in the hub. Build your personal train and the shared Mexican Train from it, matching numbers end to end.
  2. Play a domino on your turn. Tap one of your dominoes, then tap the glowing end of a train it fits. If you can't play, you draw once from the boneyard — and if you still can't play, your train is marked open and others may build on it.
  3. Cover every double. Played a double (like 9|9)? Play a second matching domino on it right away. Can't? Your train opens and the next player must try to cover it.
  4. Run out first to win the round. Everyone else scores the pips left in their hand. Lowest total score after all rounds wins the game.

What is Mexican Train dominoes?

Mexican Train Dominoes is a fun matching game played with flat tiles called dominoes. Each tile has two ends with little dots called pips. Every player starts with a hand of tiles and tries to get rid of them all first!

Why is it called a train? Players line up their tiles end to end so the matching numbers touch, looking just like a long train track leaving a station. Everyone gets their own personal train to build. There is also one extra line in the middle of the board that belongs to everybody. That shared line is the famous Mexican Train! You can read more about the history of this tile game on the Mexican Train Wikipedia page.

How do you play Mexican Train?

Learning how to play takes less than a minute. Follow these simple official rules in the game above:

  1. Start at the Engine: The game finds the highest double tile dealt to any player and puts it in the center hub. This starting tile is called the Engine.
  2. Match a number on your turn: Tap one domino in your hand that matches the open end of a train. You can build on your own train, the shared Mexican Train, or another player's train if it has an open tag.
  3. Cover every double tile: Did you play a double tile with the same number on both halves? You must play a second matching tile right away to cover it! If you cannot cover the double, your train opens up, and the next player has to cover it before anyone can make a normal move.
  4. Draw from the Boneyard when stuck: What if none of your tiles fit the board? Click the Boneyard pile to draw one new domino. If that tile still cannot play, click Pass. The game marks your train as open so other players can use it until you play on your own train again.
  5. Keep your score as low as possible: The round stops as soon as one player uses their very last tile! Everyone else counts the dots left in their hand as penalty points. The game adds up the points for you, and the player with the lowest score after all rounds wins!

Strategy

Do you want to beat the computer on Hard mode? Use these simple winning tips when you play in the tool above:

  • Dump heavy tiles early: Play tiles with lots of dots, like 12-12 or 12-11, on your very first turns. Why hold onto them? If another player finishes the round fast, those big tiles will ruin your score!
  • Keep your personal train closed: Always try to save at least one domino that fits the end of your own train. When your train turns open, your rivals will dump their unwanted tiles right onto your track!
  • Hold doubles until you can cover them: Never play a double tile unless you also have a matching single tile in your hand. Playing a double and covering it lets you get rid of two tiles in a single turn!
  • Use the shared Mexican Train wisely: Do you like the number sitting at the end of your personal train? Leave it alone and play your extra tiles on the shared Mexican Train instead!

FAQs

How many tiles to start Mexican Train dominoes?

In a classic Double-12 game for two to four players, each person starts with 12 to 15 tiles. The exact count changes based on the domino set size and player count you pick. Our online game above deals the right number of starting tiles for you automatically and places the extra tiles into the boneyard.

How does Mexican Train end?

A round ends the exact second one player places their last domino on the board! If nobody can make a move and the boneyard is empty, the round ends blocked instead. In a blocked round, the player with the lowest dot count takes zero penalty points, and everyone else adds up the dots left in their hand. After all rounds finish, the player with the lowest total score wins the match.
