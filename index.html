How to play Mexican Train Dominoes

  1. Match the engine. The highest double starts in the hub. Build your personal train and the shared Mexican Train from it, matching numbers end to end.
  2. Play a domino on your turn. Tap one of your dominoes, then tap the glowing end of a train it fits. If you can't play, you draw once from the boneyard — and if you still can't play, your train is marked open and others may build on it.
  3. Cover every double. Played a double (like 9|9)? Play a second matching domino on it right away. Can't? Your train opens and the next player must try to cover it.
  4. Run out first to win the round. Everyone else scores the pips left in their hand. Lowest total score after all rounds wins the game.

If the tiles are new to you, a round plays out like this:

  • The engine — the highest double anyone was dealt — is set in the hub, and its owner leads by building onto it.
  • Each player extends their own personal train from the hub, domino by domino, number matching number.
  • A separate Mexican Train runs through the middle of the table. It belongs to no one, and everyone may build on it.
  • Stuck with nothing to play? Take a single domino from the boneyard. Still nothing? Your train is marked with a marker and stays open — until you play on it again and shut it.
  • Score is what you avoid: when the round ends, every domino left in your hand costs you its pips.

The rules that decide who wins

  • Doubles sit sideways and must be covered. Play one and you immediately lay a second tile against it — a free bonus turn. Fail to cover and your own train swings open for the table.
  • An uncovered double is everyone's problem. The obligation to cover it passes player to player; until it's satisfied, that train can't be extended past it.
  • The round ends the instant someone goes out. If instead every player is blocked and the boneyard is dry, the lowest hand escapes penalty and everyone else counts their pips.
  • Lowest total wins the match. Classic tables play three rounds; the settings here also offer a quick single round or a five-round match.

Three habits that win rounds

  • Spend your heavy dominoes early. Every pip still in your hand at the buzzer is a penalty point waiting to happen.
  • Guard your marker. A closed train leaves rivals nowhere to dump their junk except the Mexican Train.
  • Hold doubles until you can cover them — then spend two tiles in one turn.

Questions players ask

When does a round finish?

The moment a player lays their final domino. If nobody can move and the boneyard is dry, the round ends blocked instead — and the lowest hand goes penalty-free.

How many rounds should we play?

Three rounds is the table standard and the default here. The settings also offer a single quick round or a five-round match.

Who starts, and what is the engine?

After the deal, the highest double anyone holds becomes the engine in the hub, and its owner leads by matching the engine value to open their train. Play then continues clockwise.

Is it really free — and is there anything to install?

Free, and nothing to install. It runs in any modern browser on phone, tablet or desktop, keeps working offline once loaded, and can be saved to a phone home screen like an app.

Can we play with real dominoes at a table?

Yes — the game translates straight to a kitchen table. Print the free score sheet, grab a double-twelve set, and follow the rules page step by step.

How many players does Mexican Train take?

Two to four. Play solo against the computer, or share one device with friends — each hand stays hidden until the next player taps in.
