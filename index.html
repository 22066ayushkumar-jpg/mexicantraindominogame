How to play Mexican Train Dominoes

  1. Match the engine. The highest double starts in the hub. Build your personal train and the shared Mexican Train from it, matching numbers end to end.
  2. Play a domino on your turn. Tap one of your dominoes, then tap the glowing end of a train it fits. If you can't play, you draw once from the boneyard — and if you still can't play, your train is marked open and others may build on it.
  3. Cover every double. Played a double (like 9|9)? Play a second matching domino on it right away. Can't? Your train opens and the next player must try to cover it.
  4. Run out first to win the round. Everyone else scores the pips left in their hand. Lowest total score after all rounds wins the game.

What is Mexican Train?

Mexican Train is a tile-laying dominoes game, usually played with a double-12 set of 91 tiles. A single double tile — the engine — starts in the center hub. Each player builds a personal train outward from it, and the whole table shares one extra line called the Mexican Train. A round ends when someone plays their last tile. Everyone else counts the pips left in their hand as points, and the lowest total after the final round wins.

The name comes from the long line of tiles, which looks like a train pulling out of a station. House rules differ from table to table, so this version follows the most common setup: the highest double dealt becomes the engine, doubles must be covered, and a train opens when its owner cannot play. You can compare descriptions in the Wikipedia entry for Mexican Train, check rule variants in Pagat's Mexican Train rules overview, or read our complete Mexican Train rules guide.

The engine, the trains, and the boneyard

  • Engine: the double that starts the board. Here it is the highest double dealt to any player, and that player takes the first turn.
  • Personal train: your own line. Other players can only use it once it is open.
  • Mexican Train: the shared line in the middle. It is public for the whole round.
  • Boneyard: the face-down pile. You draw one tile from it when you cannot play.

How to play in your browser

  1. Start a game. The board above deals automatically. Open New Game to change the domino set, opponents, or number of rounds.
  2. Play on the engine value. The first tile on every train must match the engine number.
  3. Match an open end. Tap a tile, then tap a glowing train end. You can play on your own train, the Mexican Train, or any train that is open.
  4. Cover your doubles. A double must be covered with a matching tile immediately. If you cannot, you draw once and the obligation passes to the next player.
  5. Draw, then pass. With no legal move, draw one tile from the boneyard. If that still does not fit, pass and your train opens.
  6. Finish first. The first player out scores nothing; the rest count the pips in their hand. Lowest total after 1, 3, or 5 rounds wins.

Mexican Train rules worth knowing

  • You must play if you can. Passing is only allowed when you have no legal move, and only after drawing.
  • Doubles block the table. While a double is uncovered, no other train can be extended until someone covers it.
  • Open trains stay open until their owner plays on them. That is the cost of passing: rivals can dump high tiles on your line.
  • The lowest score wins. Winning a round is worth 0 points, but one bad round of heavy tiles can cost you the match.

These match the rules used by the engine above. Variations such as fixed engine rounds, no-draw play, and partner games are covered on the Mexican Train variations page.

Tips that actually matter

  • Shed high pips early. A 12–12 or 12–11 left in your hand can decide the match.
  • Keep a play for your own train. As long as you can add to your train, it stays private.
  • Do not play a double unless you can cover it. Playing and covering in one turn removes two tiles; failing opens your train.
  • Use the Mexican Train for orphan tiles. Park numbers you cannot extend instead of opening your own line.

FAQ

Can I play Mexican Train without downloading or signing up?

Yes. The game loads in your browser and starts immediately — no account, no email, no install. You can install it to your home screen if you want an app-like icon, but that is optional.

How many dominoes do you start with in Mexican Train?

It depends on the set. With 2 to 4 players, double-6 deals 7 tiles each, double-9 deals 10, double-12 deals 13, and double-15 deals 15. The remaining tiles form the boneyard. This game deals and tracks them automatically.

How does a Mexican Train round end?

Two ways: one player empties their hand and scores 0, or every player is blocked with an empty boneyard, in which case the lowest pip count scores 0. Everyone else adds up the pips left in their hand. Lowest total after the final round wins.

Can two people play Mexican Train on one phone?

Yes. Choose 2, 3, or 4 human players in New Game Settings and pass the device between turns. A privacy screen hides each hand. You can also mix human and computer players, up to 4 in total.

Does this Mexican Train game work offline on mobile?

Yes. After the first load the game runs offline and saves your progress in the browser. On iPhone, use Share then Add to Home Screen; on Android, use Install app.

Is Mexican Train the same as Chicken Foot?

No. Both start from a central double, but in Chicken Foot a double needs three tiles to close and splits the line into a foot shape. In Mexican Train a double needs one tile, and each player builds a single straight train. Our Chicken Foot vs Mexican Train comparison covers the scoring differences.
