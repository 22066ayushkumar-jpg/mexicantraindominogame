How to play Mexican Train Dominoes

  1. Match the engine. The highest double starts in the hub. Build your personal train and the shared Mexican Train from it, matching numbers end to end.
  2. Play a domino on your turn. Tap one of your dominoes, then tap the glowing end of a train it fits. If you can't play, you draw once from the boneyard - and if you still can't play, your train is marked open and others may build on it.
  3. Cover every double. Played a double (like 9|9)? Play a second matching domino on it right away. Can't? Your train opens and the next player must try to cover it.
  4. Run out first to win the round. Everyone else scores the pips left in their hand. Lowest total score after all rounds wins the game.

What is the Mexican Train Dominoes Game?

Mexican Train dominoes game is a tile-laying game played with a set of dominoes. The goal is simple: be the first player to play all your dominoes. A central train called the Mexican Train belongs to everyone and anyone can add tiles to it. Each player also has a personal train that starts from the hub. Numbers must match end to end to play a tile. When you cannot play you draw one domino from the boneyard. If you still cannot play after drawing your train becomes open and other players may build on it. The round ends when one player goes out or when the boneyard is empty and all players are blocked.

How to Play Mexican Train Dominoes?

To start a game of Mexican Train dominoes the highest double tile dealt becomes the engine in the hub. The player who holds it makes the first move by placing a tile that matches the engine number. Play then proceeds clockwise. On your turn you may play a domino from your hand that matches an open end on any train. If you have no matching tile draw one domino from the boneyard. If you still have no play mark your train as open and the turn passes to the next player.

Doubles are special in Mexican Train dominoes game! When you play a double you must immediately play a second tile against it -- this is called covering the double. If you cannot cover the double your train opens and other players may play on it. The Mexican Train itself is always open to all players from the very first turn!

The game continues until one player plays their final domino and goes out. That player wins the round. All other players count the pips dots remaining in their hand. The player with the lowest total score after all rounds wins the overall game.

What are the rules for playing Mexican Train in dominoes?

Mexican Train dominoes has a few key rules that make the game flow smoothly. First the engine double is placed in the hub at the start and its owner leads the first turn. Second each player builds their own train radiating from the hub. Third the Mexican Train in the center is public any player may add tiles to it at any time.

Fourth doubles must be covered immediately. If a player plays a double and cannot cover it their train becomes open for the rest of the game. Fifth if a player has no playable tile they draw one from the boneyard. If the drawn tile still does not match they must mark their train as open.

Sixth a round ends when one player goes out plays their last domino or when the boneyard is empty and no player can make a legal move. In the blocked situation the player with the lowest pip count in hand wins the round. Seventh the overall winner is the player with the lowest cumulative score after the agreed number of rounds typically three.

What is the best strategy to win Mexican Train Dominoes?

To win at Mexican Train dominoes game focus on getting rid of your high-pip tiles early in the round. Heavy tiles like double-fives or double-blanks count many points if they remain in your hand at the end. Play them while you still have opportunities.

Second try to keep your train closed as long as possible. An open train lets opponents dump their unwanted tiles on it which can backfire later. Only open your train when you have no viable moves left.

Third save your doubles for strategic moments. A well-timed double can block opponents or give you a free extra turn if you can cover it immediately. Holding doubles too long risks being stuck with them when the round ends.

Fourth pay attention to the Mexican Train! If it grows quickly other players may be forced to play there rather than on their personal trains this can work to your advantage. Finally watch which tiles have been played and track which ones are still likely in play so you can plan your moves several turns ahead!

Faqs:

How many dominoes are in a Mexican Train game?

A standard Mexican Train domino game uses a double-12 set which contains 91 dominoes total. If you play with a double-6 set there are 28 dominoes double-9 has 55 and double-15 has 136. The number of tiles each player starts with depends on the set size and player count typically 15 for double-12 with 4 players 10 for double-9 and 7 for double-6.

Is this Mexican Train dominoes 12 or 15?

This Mexican Train dominoes game supports both double-12 and double-15 sets. The default and most popular choice is double-12 with 91 dominoes because it offers a good balance of game length and variety. Double-15 sets have 136 tiles and make for longer more complex matches. You can switch between set sizes in the game settings before starting a new round.

When does a round end?

A round of Mexican Train dominoes ends the moment a player plays their very last domino and goes out. If no player can go out and the boneyard is also empty with no legal moves available the round ends as a blocked round. In a blocked round the player with the lowest total pips in their hand wins that round and scores zero while everyone else counts their remaining pips as penalty points.

How does the first round start?

The first round of Mexican Train dominoes begins with the deal. Each player receives their allotted number of tiles based on the set size and player count. After the tiles are dealt each player looks at their hand for the highest double. The player who holds the highest double places it in the hub to start the game. That player then makes the first move by matching a tile to the engine double.

How is Mexican Train different from Dominoes?

Mexican Train dominoes game differs from basic block or draw dominoes in a few key ways. The most visible difference is the central Mexican Train that belongs to all players in standard domino games each player typically has only their own train. Mexican Train also has special rules for doubles that require immediate covering and the game uses a hub-and-spoke layout with the engine double at the center.
