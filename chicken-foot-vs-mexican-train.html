Chicken Foot vs Mexican Train: Rules, Scoring & Key Differences

When you compare chicken foot vs mexican train, you are looking at two of the best dominoes games in the world. Both games start with a single double tile right in the middle of the table, and your main goal is to get rid of all the tiles in your hand first. So, what is the big secret in the chicken foot vs mexican train battle? Learn the rules of chicken foot vs mexican train dominoes. See which game is easier, how to score points, and how to win your next family game night!

The biggest difference is how the tiles look on the table. In Chicken Foot, every double tile forces players to build a three-toed bird foot shape together! In Mexican Train, each person builds their own single, straight line of tiles. Both games are super fun for the whole family!

  • Layout & Shape: Chicken Foot uses shared lines that grow out from the middle hub. Every time someone plays a double, the line splits into three new paths. It looks like a giant spiderweb by the end of the round! Mexican Train is much neater. Each player builds their own private line going out from the center station, plus there is one extra line that everyone can share.
  • Doubles Rules: Doubles change everything! In Chicken Foot, when a player puts down a double tile, they call out "Chicken Foot!" Now, nobody can play anywhere else on the board until three matching tiles are placed against that double. In Mexican Train, a double only needs one matching tile on top of it to satisfy the rule.
  • Starting Hands: How many tiles do you draw to start? It depends on how many friends are playing and if you use a Double-9 or Double-12 set. In Chicken Foot, players usually pull 7 to 15 tiles at the start of a round. In Mexican Train, you normally draw 10 to 15 tiles to begin your turn.
  • Terminology: The words you use during the game are totally different. In Chicken Foot, the pile of leftover tiles is called the Chicken Yard, and the three branches on a double are called toes. In Mexican Train, you pull extra tiles from the Boneyard, the center tile is the Engine, and you put a tiny plastic train marker on your line when you get stuck.
  • Scoring: In both games, you count the dots on the tiles left in your hand when the round ends, and each dot equals one point. You want zero points! However, watch out for the double-blank tile (0-0). In Mexican Train, it is worth zero points. In Chicken Foot rules, getting stuck with that blank tile adds a whopping 50 points to your score!

Rules: Chicken Foot vs Mexican Train

You must follow a few strict rules to keep the game fair. Do not break these!

  • The Draw Rule: In both games, you are only allowed to draw one tile from the pile if you do not have a matching tile in your hand. You cannot draw just because you want a better tile!
  • Closing Doubles: In Chicken Foot, a double blocks the whole board for everyone until three tiles are played on it. In Mexican Train, an open double blocks all trains too, and the next player must play on that double before doing anything else.
  • Train Markers: Mexican Train uses little plastic markers, or even pennies, to show when a line is open. If you cannot play a tile after drawing from the Boneyard, you must put a marker on your train. Your marker stays there until you finally play a tile on your own line on a later turn. Chicken Foot does not use markers at all because every line belongs to everyone.
  • Calling Last Tile: In many homes, you have to tap your last tile on the table or say "Uno" when you only have one tile left. Ask your friends if they use this rule before you start!

How to Play: Chicken Foot vs Mexican Train

Setting up both games is a breeze. First, flip all the dominoes face down and mix them up well. Each player takes their starting tiles and stands them up so nobody else can peek.

Playing Chicken Foot:

  1. The player with the highest double puts it in the center of the table.
  2. Players take turns putting matching tiles on all four sides of that center double.
  3. After all four sides are full, players can add tiles to the ends of those lines.
  4. When someone plays a double sideways, the next three turns must go onto that double to make a three-toed foot.
  5. If you cannot play a tile, draw one from the Chicken Yard. Play it right away if it fits, or pass your turn!

Playing Mexican Train:

  1. Put the required double tile in the center hub to act as the Engine for that round.
  2. On your first turn, start your own personal train going out from the Engine.
  3. On each turn after that, play one tile on your own train, on the shared Mexican Train, or on another player's train if it has a marker on it.
  4. If you play a double, you must play one more tile right away to cover it. If you cannot cover it, you have to draw a tile and put a marker on your train!
  5. Keep going until one person runs out of tiles.

Strategy: Chicken Foot vs Mexican Train

Want to win every single time? You have to change how you think for each game.

In Chicken Foot, doubles are your best weapon. Play a double when you know other players do not have that number in their hands. That forces them to dig into the Chicken Yard! Also, get rid of the double-blank tile as fast as you can. You do not want 50 bad points at the end!

In Mexican Train, the smart move is to build a long chain of tiles in your hand before the game even starts. Keep your own personal line closed to other players for as long as possible. If you cannot play a tile on your turn, you have to put a marker on your train, and then everyone can play on your line! Play your highest dot tiles early on the shared Mexican Train so you do not get stuck holding big numbers.

How to Declare a Winner: Chicken Foot vs Mexican Train

How do you know who wins the whole game? Both games play through several rounds, starting with the highest double (like 12-12) and working all the way down to the 0-0 tile.

A single round ends the second one player puts down their very last tile. That lucky player gets zero points for the round! Sometimes, the board gets completely blocked and nobody can make a move, even when the draw pile is empty. When that happens, the round just stops right there.

Once a round stops, everyone counts the little dots on the tiles still left in their hands. Write those numbers down on a piece of paper. After you finish the final 0-0 round, add up all the points from every round. The player with the lowest total score is the grand champion!
