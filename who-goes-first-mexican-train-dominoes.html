Who Goes First in Mexican Train Dominoes?

The player holding the highest designated double tile, called the engine, goes first in Mexican Train Dominoes. In a standard match played with a Double-12 set, whoever holds the 12-12 places it in the center hub and takes the opening turn.

Placing the engine does not count as your move. You immediately take your regular turn by laying down a matching 12 to start either your personal train or the communal Mexican Train. After that first move is complete, play passes clockwise to the next person.

What If You Hold the Engine but Cannot Match It?

Setting the engine is mandatory, but you might not have a second tile that matches it. If that happens:

  1. Place the 12-12 in the center hub.
  2. Draw a single tile from the boneyard.
  3. If the drawn domino has a 12 on one end, play it immediately.
  4. If it does not match, your turn ends. Place your train marker (or a penny) on your personal track to mark your train open to everyone else. Play then moves clockwise.

What Happens If Nobody Drew the Double-12?

If the 12-12 remains hidden in the boneyard after the initial deal:

  • Starting to the left of the dealer, players take turns drawing one tile from the boneyard in clockwise order.
  • The moment someone pulls the 12-12, that player places it directly into the center hub.
  • That same player then takes their opening turn, and normal play continues clockwise from their position.

Who Starts Subsequent Rounds?

Mexican Train consists of multiple rounds that count down sequentially through every double tile:

  • Round 1 begins with the 12-12
  • Round 2 begins with the 11-11
  • Round 3 begins with the 10-10
  • The pattern continues down to Round 13, which opens with the double-blank (0-0)

In every round, whoever is dealt that round's specific double places the engine and begins play. If your group uses a Double-9 or Double-15 set, start with the 9-9 or 15-15 instead and work down from there. Larger and smaller sets change the number of rounds as well as the starting tile — the Mexican Train variations guide covers the alternatives.

Common House Rule to Check Before Playing

Some casual groups separate placing the engine from the first turn. Under this variation, whoever holds the required double places it in the center hub, but the player seated to their left takes the first actual play. Both methods are widely used, so clarify which rule your table follows before shuffling the dominoes.
